## What we learned
- **trail+gate is the best variant on every measure, but it is not proven.**
  - In both periods its returns, Sharpe, fee share (26–34% of pre-fee profit vs 49% for base) and profit concentration are better than base.
  - All 12 neighbouring settings are positive.
  - It fails the significance bar narrowly (t 1.96).
  - It fails the cost-stress test on H1, and that failure matters: if real fees are ≥ 0.2%, it doesn't survive.
- **The BTC gate helps consistently.** On its own it improved H1 (2.85 → 4.33) and the research period (2.01 → 2.46), and it raised Sharpe in both.
- **The trailing exit on its own is mixed.** It's better in the falling 2025–26 market and worse in 2021–24, so it should only be used together with the gate.
- **Random-entry benchmark, two definitions:**
  - Random timing across the *whole* period: every variant beats it, by about 0.2R per trade. The strategy's value is in being long during up-moves.
  - Random timing *within the same months* (the audit's version): breakout ranks at only the 72nd–93rd percentile.

  So the edge is regime timing, not precise entry timing. That fits the BTC-gate result.
- **Costs are the binding constraint.** Every variant lost money on H1 at 0.2% fee + 0.05% slippage.

## Consequences
- Per the protocol, nothing is promoted and H2 stays unused.
- Not allowed: tuning trail+gate further on these same two periods and re-testing. That would be the overfitting the protocol exists to prevent.
- Legitimate next steps:
  1. collect *new* evidence by tracking trail+gate in the 4h paper experiment as an **unvalidated shadow** configuration (needs the owner's OK)
  2. lower costs (limit entries)
  3. learn the owner's real fee tier, which determines whether gate 4 is even the relevant hurdle.

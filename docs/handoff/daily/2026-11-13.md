## What was asked
Build a bot that trades and scalps using several methods with minimal risk. Paper trade it on real-time data,
stress test and keep improving it, and report daily on how it did.

## What was done today
- Built a fake-money scalping bot (`lib/paper/scalp/`). Methods:
  - dip-buy (Bollinger + RSI)
  - breakout
  - trend pullback
  - VWAP snap-back
- Timeframes are 1m to 60m. Each method runs buy-only or buy + short.
- Risk limits:
  - 0.5% risk per trade, no leverage
  - fee and stop filters
  - 2% daily loss stop
  - cool-down after 3 losses in a row
  - 10% kill switch
- Tested it on 30 days of real 1-minute Crypto.com prices for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP and DOGE (`scripts/paper/research.ts`).
  Settings were tuned on 10 days across all coins, then tested on the next 5 days.
- Stress tests: fees 2.5× higher, slippage 3× higher, top 10% of trades removed, shuffled trade order.
- Started a live forward paper test (`scripts/paper/live.ts`):
  - $10,000 fake money in 25 pots of $400 (5 methods × 5 coins)
  - settings frozen
  - clock started 2026-11-13 05:17 UTC
- Created the cloud environment `empire-`, which allows only `api.crypto.com` (public prices, no keys). Node needs `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1`.
- 15 unit tests pass. Everything is committed and pushed.

## Key findings
- On days it wasn't tuned on, no method reliably made money. The best were about break-even (profit factor 0.92–1.05).
  It was a falling month: the coins dropped 15–24%. The bot lost only about 0–2% per coin.
- 1-minute scalping loses at about 0.1% fees: costs (about 0.24% round trip) are bigger than a typical 1-minute move.
- The biggest improvement was the cost filters. They cut losses from about 10–20% to about 0–2% per coin.
- The simulator is fair: random trades with zero costs come out flat.
- Live bid/ask spreads are tiny (under 0.02%), so the slippage assumption is conservative.
